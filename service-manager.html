Service Manager Operating System

I worked with a field-service manager to redesign how operational priorities, urgent issues and unfinished commitments were organized. I translated the realities of his role into business rules, then configured Google Workspace, Gmail, Gemini and Workspace Studio workflows around ownership, escalation and management follow-through.

Explore the working demonstrationRead the project brief

The business challenge

Keeping operational responsibility visible when the manager is in the field.

Customer escalations, technicians, vendor decisions, service scheduling, national accounts and leadership requests compete for attention throughout a service manager's day. That work does not stop when the manager steps away from the computer.

Unread messages and conventional inbox folders do not show who owns the next decision, whether an obligation is still active, or which exception needs immediate attention. A reply can be mistaken for closure while the underlying work remains unfinished.

I approached this as an operational accountability problem, not an inbox-cleanup exercise. The objective was a system that represents current responsibilities and turns communication into useful management decisions.

My contribution

I assessed the manager's responsibilities, identified key stakeholder and issue categories, defined a state and priority model, configured AI-assisted workflow components and revised the logic as implementation constraints surfaced.

The operating system

Five connected capabilities, built around management work.

01 · Discovery and requirements

Map the actual obligations

Separated business-critical issues from routine information. Considered national accounts, customer escalations, vendors, technicians, schedulers, leadership requests and time-sensitive decisions. Converted these priorities into explicit workflow requirements.

02 · Work-state architecture

Represent the current responsibility

Designed exclusive ACTION, WAITING, FOLLOW_UP, INFO, RESOLVED and NEEDS_REVIEW states. Preserved customer and vendor category labels, while keeping urgency distinct from ownership and completion.

03 · AI-assisted classification

Use interpretation with operating rules

Configured Gmail, Gemini and Workspace Studio components to assess communication context and propose an appropriate category, work state and priority. Business rules define safe handling; uncertain ownership routes to human review.

04 · Escalation workflow

Surface the exceptions that matter

Structured an independent urgent path using 00 - URGENT, work-state labels and Google Chat alerting. An urgent matter can remain urgent while the manager waits for another person's response.

05 · Management decision support

Make the open work actionable

Designed a morning brief, a midday field checkpoint and an end-of-day open-loops review. The management questions are: what needs attention, who owns it, what happens next, and what remains unresolved?

Operational conventions

Familiar, precise work queues

Preserved practical Gmail labels such as 01 - ACTION - YOU and 02 - WAITING - THEM. They support quick visual scanning without treating a label, a new email or a manager reply as proof that work is finished.

Six current work states

ACTION

The manager or identified owner still owes a decision, response or operational action.

WAITING

A confirmed third party has the next action. The dependency remains visible, not silently closed.

FOLLOW_UP

The manager must revisit a matter at a defined checkpoint or verify later progress.

INFO

Useful information with no current operational obligation.

RESOLVED

The obligation has actually been completed and its closure verified.

NEEDS_REVIEW

Missing context, conflicting instructions or unclear ownership require human judgment.

Business decisions

Three decisions shaped the solution.

Decision 01

Ownership beats email activity

Problem: An outgoing reply could incorrectly move active work into WAITING or RESOLVED.

Decision: Evaluate the current obligation and relevant thread context before changing status. If the manager says, “I will inspect tomorrow, then decide,” the manager still owns ACTION.

Operational purpose: Avoid losing track of unfinished commitments.

Decision 02

Urgency is not a work state

Problem: An issue can remain business-critical while its next step belongs to a vendor or technician.

Decision: Maintain urgency independently of ACTION, WAITING or any other status.

Operational purpose: Keep risk and escalation visible across ownership changes.

Decision 03

Manager attention beats inbox volume

Problem: Long digests compete with field work instead of helping prioritize it.

Decision: Design three action-focused management checkpoints around priorities, responsibility and unresolved items.

Operational purpose: Create a useful rhythm for decisions and follow-through.

Implementation and troubleshooting

Working through actual platform constraints.

The hands-on work involved more than writing classification prompts. Configuration had to accommodate Gmail labels, Workspace Studio triggers, missing variables, message context and the risks of changing live work states.

Label conflicts and duplicate states

Legacy and duplicate labels risked leaving contradictory ownership signals. I refined the conventions toward one current work state per obligation while preserving useful customer, vendor and source labels.

Reply-based misclassification

A newest-message shortcut could clear work prematurely. I refined the rule to evaluate outstanding responsibilities and introduce review where thread context or ownership was insufficient.

Studio limits and trigger coverage

Step-count constraints, missing thread-identification variables and inbox-bypass behavior affected workflow construction. I revised configurations and defined fallback or review paths rather than assuming every trigger was available.

Human override and exceptions

Ambiguous ownership, repeated triggers and manual correction required a cautious design. The intended safeguards preserve an active obligation instead of guessing at completion or autonomously sending, archiving or deleting messages.
