Portfolio / AI Workflow Design

Independent simulation | AI adoption and human oversight

AI should clear the work, not blur who's responsible for it.

I designed a synthetic service workflow combining structured AI interpretation, deterministic operating rules and human review. The aim is less administrative friction without turning consequential business decisions over to an unchecked model.

The business problem and my approach

Technicians often describe exceptions in free text. A manager still needs an accurate category, next owner and follow-up. A language model may assist with summarizing and choosing from approved options, but it should not silently make changes.

What I worked on: use-case selection, structured-output design, decision boundaries, safety controls, evaluation scenarios and fallback procedures. The later Transformation Lab implementation carries these principles into executable workflow logic.

Service-note decision flow

  1. Interpret the incoming service note and identify the active obligation.
  2. Apply scheduling, safety and qualification rules outside the language model.
  3. Route ambiguous or high-impact actions to an authorized reviewer.
  4. Record the approved disposition and preserve follow-up ownership.

Read the workflow design brief

Automation needs boundaries, ownership and a way back.

1. Keep firm rules outside the model

Scheduling eligibility, technician capacity, consent and safety triggers should use deterministic checks. AI suggestions cannot override them.

2. Constrain the output

The assistant could propose a short summary, an allowed exception category, a follow-up type and an approved knowledge-base reference. It should abstain when it lacks enough information.

3. Require review before action

A supervisor or dispatcher checks the source note and any suggested label. The AI has no authority to send messages, change routes, grant consent or close an issue.

4. Preserve manual fallback

If the AI response is unclear, invalid, inconsistent with a safety rule or unavailable, staff use the existing structured exception process.

What should be tested before any pilot

The test plan separates ordinary cases, ambiguous language, instructions hidden in a note, and safety-sensitive reports. Each test needs a retained input, expected result, model/version, actual output, reviewer decision and failure disposition before performance can be calculated.

Evaluation approach

The original design specifies representative cases, expected decisions, safety boundaries and review procedures. The Transformation Lab extends this approach with executable synthetic workflows, retained outputs and versioned technical evidence.

Applied controls and evaluation

Build the controls around the model, not the other way around.

The expanded Juniper Vale lab adds testable synthetic incident intake, deterministic replay, business-rule gates, manager approvals and a traceable simulated execution path. The current replay is not presented as a live language model.
